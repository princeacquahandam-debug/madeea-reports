How Claude and Grokbot took the Hub from "Not ready" to "Ready"

Between 20 and 24 November, Grokbot ran six rounds of testing on the live Hub, and Claude fixed what each round found. The Hub went from "Not ready" to "Ready", both for viewing pages and for saving and sending. Nothing went live until it had been checked and approved.

1

Who did what

Grokbot found the problems; Claude found their causes, fixed them and checked the fixes; nothing went live until it had been checked and someone said "push it".

  • Grokbot (the tester): an AI agent set up on 20 November with a written QA-tester brief. It maps the site, tests pages and actions with real accounts, and reports each problem with exact steps, a screenshot and a severity (Critical, High, Medium, Low). It never shows a password.
  • Claude (the builder): read each report, reproduced the problems, traced each to its cause in the code or database, fixed it, tested the fix (often by simulating the problem, such as a dropped connection), wrote the re-test brief for Grokbot, and checked each change on the live site after it went out.
  • The team: sent Grokbot the briefs and passwords, checked each fix on a local copy, ran the database updates in Supabase, and decided what to build and when to publish.

How testing was kept safe

No real client, message or record was touched by a test: rounds 1 to 3 only read, and rounds 4 to 6 ran in a separate team that cannot see real data.

  • The QA Sandbox: a second team inside the live Hub with its own owner, assistant and test client. The tester confirmed in every session that no real name or client ever appeared.
  • Test accounts: "+qa" versions of existing Gmail addresses (QA Owner, QA EA, QA Client), so their mail still reaches inboxes we control. Passwords are kept privately and were sent to Grokbot separately from each brief.
  • Grokbot's rules: only the sandbox accounts for anything that saves; everything created starts with "QA-"; invite or send only to "+qa" addresses; no integrations or alerts switched on; at most 10 AI requests a round; never show a password.
  • Screenshots are off for the sandbox only, because Grokbot's browser cannot show Chrome's screen-share window; the real team keeps them on.
  • Clean-up so far: two leftover test logins deleted, and the access tokens used for deployments deleted afterwards. The QA- tasks, messages and shifts are still in the sandbox, harmless and only visible there.

Still open, and the next test

Nothing from Grokbot's reports is still failing; what remains are a few items outside the Hub's code.

  • madeea.io emails to madeeas.com: they can land in Spam, or arrive with a red "might be dangerous" warning because madeea.io looks like madeeas.com. Personal Gmail filters work now; the full fix is two settings in the madeeas.com Google Admin console (request ready to send).
  • End-of-day email to the team leads: the n8n workflow is built and its email checked; setting it up in n8n, Supabase and the Hub is in progress.
  • Sandbox tidy-up (optional): remove the QA- tasks, messages and shifts before the next round.

To run the next test: write a short brief listing only what changed (as in round 6), paste it into Grokbot, then send the three QA passwords separately. Before each release, re-running round 4's summary table in the sandbox is a quick way to catch anything that broke.
