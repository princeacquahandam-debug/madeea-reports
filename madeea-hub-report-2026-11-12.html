1Live

The founder's organisation comes first

Abder's tree: the founder at the top, then the EA and everyone who reports directly to the founder. As Rowena asked, the client fills it in before using the coach.

  • The first time a client opens Delegate, it asks "Before you start: who reports to you?"
  • For each person: name, title, area (finance, product, commercial and so on), what they handle, and their MadeEA login if they have one.
  • It shows as a tree: the founder, then the EA, then their direct reports. It can be edited at any time, or skipped for now.
2Live

Keep or delegate, sorted every day

Prince's point: a founder should focus on a few things every day, and everything else should go to the right person. The coach now works from what the platform already knows, as Abder described.

  • The founder names up to 4 focus areas and keeps a list of what is on their desk.
  • It adds the founder's meetings for the next 7 days from their connected calendar.
  • Once a day, when the founder opens Delegate, Madeline sorts every item into keep, the EA, a named person in the organisation, or a role nobody covers yet, each with a one-line reason.
  • Follow-ups, emails, scheduling and chasing go to the EA. Work for a department goes to the person who covers it.
  • Meetings that name the founder, and one-to-ones, stay with the founder. Trainings and events are never sent to someone else.
3Live

What the EA can take until a role exists

Rowena's point: when a task belongs to a department the business doesn't have yet, finance for example, the coach doesn't stop at "hire someone". It shows "Until then, [EA] can: …". That is the part the EA can take now, such as gathering documents, chasing or keeping a tracker. That part can be handed to the EA in one click. Where it fits, it also suggests the role the business may need.

4Live

Handed over in one click, to the right person

  • One click: "Hand to [name]" puts the item on that person's task board, with the coach's reason in the notes. The item then shows "Handed to [name]" and comes off the founder's desk list.
  • Not only the EA: work can go to anyone in the organisation who has a MadeEA login.
  • People not on MadeEA: "Plan it first" builds a full delegation plan with a handoff message the founder copies and sends. The plan is kept as handed over.
  • Full plans still work for any item: outcome, context, how much freedom, success criteria, risks and the handoff message.
5Fixed

Client AI working again

While building this, we found that the AI cost limits added on 7 November also blocked client accounts. From 7 to 12 November, a client using Madeline in the client portal was told they had reached today's AI limit. That is fixed. Clients now have the same daily allowance as the team: 150 AI requests or $0.50 a day per person.

7Live

The Hub on hub.madeea.io

The Hub now has its own address on the MadeEA domain: hub.madeea.io, with a secure connection.

  • The old addresses, hub.madeeas.com and madeea-hub.vercel.app, still work, so nobody is locked out during the switch.
  • The madeea.io website, n8n and madeea.io email were not touched. Only a new "hub" record was added.
  • Sign-in, password-reset links and Google connections all use the new address. Gmail was disconnected and connected again through hub.madeea.io to prove it works.
8Approved

Google sign-in approved as MadeEA Hub

When someone connects their Google account, Google's screen now shows MadeEA Hub with links to our home page, privacy policy and terms, instead of a technical address.

  • Google confirmed that MadeEA owns madeea.io.
  • Google's first check said the privacy policy page had too little content. It could not read it, because the page was drawn by the app. The privacy policy and terms are now plain public pages at hub.madeea.io/privacy and hub.madeea.io/terms.
  • Both pages were also finished: the "draft" banner and blank placeholders are gone, they name MadeEA Hub and give a contact email, and the privacy policy lists the services we use and how Google data is used, kept and deleted.
  • Google re-checked and approved the branding the same day, and it is now shown to users.
9Next week

Google's review of Gmail and Calendar access

Because the Hub reads Gmail, Google reviews how it uses that data before removing the "unverified app" warning. Everything for that review is ready except the demo video.

  • The three permissions the Hub uses are listed: read Gmail, send Gmail, and calendar events. Each has an explanation of why it is needed.
  • Next week: record a 2 to 3 minute demo video of connecting Google, the Inbox, sending a reply and the calendar, then submit for review.
  • After that: Google asks for a yearly security assessment (CASA) by an outside lab, because reading Gmail is a restricted permission. It is paid, roughly USD 500 to 4,500.
  • Until then, Gmail keeps working for up to 100 users. They see Google's "unverified app" warning when connecting.
