The team told the Academy is open

The message announcing the Academy went to the team: how to open it (Learn → MadeEA Academy in the Hub, or academy.madeea.io with their Hub sign-in), what Made Ready covers, how the flashcards and quizzes work, and who reviews the practical tasks. Everyone on the team can start Made Ready now.

2Decision

Day 1 tested as a learner, and one gap

Nobody had yet taken the course as a learner: the client's review covered the content but skipped the exercises. So Day 1 was taken from start to finish with a learner account.

  • Working: every lesson, the flashcards, and each module quiz, which marks answers straight away ("7 of 10 right · not yet" below the 80% pass mark, "passed" above it) and allows a retry. Day 1 showed as complete, the Made Ready path moved to 36%, Day 2 and Day 3 were ready to start, and the course review saved.
  • The gap: a day counts as finished once its lessons are read, whether or not the quizzes were passed. And because the Made Ready path has no final exam, the certificate unlocks after reading every lesson. In the old Training Center, Made Ready needed every quiz passed at 80% and the practical tasks reviewed.
  • The fix, ready to build: turn on the path's final exam (80% pass mark, a set of questions per attempt, a time limit and a number of attempts) and fill its question bank from the 64 quiz questions the course already has. No code change is needed. This is a decision for Prince and Abder, together with whether practical tasks should be submitted and reviewed in the Academy.
3Live

The course review, and a new scenario lesson

A colleague who finished every course wrote a review. It was very positive: the lessons were detailed and easy to follow, the focus on supporting a client well stood out, the AI lessons were practical (especially on when to use your own judgment), and the questions helped check understanding. They would recommend it to anyone preparing for a first client.

It had one suggestion: a short scenario where a learner has to prioritise several client requests and explain what they'd handle first. That is now a lesson:

  • "Scenario: five requests before 10 AM", in Day 1 right after "Task prioritization framework". A CEO's morning brings five requests: a board deck error, an investor reschedule, a client invoice, an offsite booking and a flight fare that rises at noon.
  • The learner writes their order and reasoning first, then compares it with a worked answer and what makes an answer strong.
  • Tested on a copy before it went in. Made Ready now has 48 lessons, with Day 1 at 18.
