The client portal, end to end

Today the client portal went from a page that showed blank screens to a full workspace. Clients see their own calendar, connect Google or Outlook, and can share their Outlook mail with their EA. Their own staff get a proper working space. Everything below is live at hub.madeeas.com and was tested on the live site after each release.

1Live

Client portal: blank pages fixed, everything the Hub had, plus Madeline

Several portal pages looked empty: the content was there, but the page's background was drawn over it. That's fixed everywhere in the portal. Then everything the client side was missing compared with the Hub was added:

  • A richer Overview: a one-line summary of where the account stands, hours this week, what's coming up and what was done today.
  • Tasks as a board, by status, so a client sees their work the way their EA does.
  • Daily reports from their EA: done, blocked, and next.
  • Header tools: search, a short guide, light/dark theme and notifications.
  • Madeline in the portal. She answers from the client's own account only (tasks, meetings, hours, shared notes) and can turn a request into a task for their EA in one click.
  • Every page works on a phone.
2Live

Set a client's Lead EA, and the right name for Prince

The portal says "Your assistant, …", but there was no way to choose who that is. Client Vault → Edit client now has a Lead EA picker. The Lead EA is also who sees the client's calendar and, if the client agrees, their mail (items 4 and 6).

Prince's name showed as "Princeacquahandam". It now reads Prince Acquah Andam everywhere, including his past daily reports, and it stays correct even if his account is re-invited.

3Live

A real calendar in the client portal

The portal calendar was a plain list. It now uses the same Month and Week views as the Hub, with Agenda as a third view and the default on phones. Clicking a meeting opens its details, with a Join the call button when there's a video link. The client's choice of view is remembered.

4Live

Clients connect their Google or Outlook / Teams calendar

A new Connected accounts page lets a client connect their own calendar, from Google or Outlook / Microsoft 365. Teams meetings come through with their Join link. Their real schedule then shows in their portal, and their Lead EA sees it in the Hub Calendar, labelled with the client's name, so they can plan the day and book around it.

  • Who sees it: the client, their Lead EA and MadeEA admins. Not the rest of the team.
  • Calendar only. Connecting a calendar never gives access to email.
  • It refreshes itself when the client opens their calendar, and a moved or cancelled meeting disappears on the next refresh.
  • Disconnect removes every one of their events from MadeEA.
  • Only the client's primary contact can connect accounts. Viewers and staff can't.

Also fixed: Google meetings weren't keeping their Meet links, so there was no Join button. They now keep them.

6Live

Clients share their Outlook mail with their EA

A client can now let their Lead EA handle their Outlook inbox. On Connected accounts they click Share my Outlook mail with [their EA], and Microsoft asks for their permission. In the Hub, a Client mailboxes button appears in the Communication Center, where the EA can read the client's mail, reply as the client, or send a new email from the client's address.

What the client is promised, and what the system enforces

  • Only their EA can see their mail. Not other staff, not admins, and not MadeEA's AI tools. Their mail is kept apart from everything else in the Hub.
  • A "Sent on your behalf" list shows every email the EA sent or replied to, with who sent it, to whom and when.
  • Nothing changes in their Outlook: nothing is marked read or deleted.
  • Shared with a person, not a role: if MadeEA changes their EA, sharing pauses until the client agrees again.
  • Stop sharing at any time. Access ends at once, and MadeEA's copies of their mail are deleted.
Gmail next The same for Gmail is ready to build, but Google requires a paid security assessment (CASA) before any app can read client Gmail. Outlook went first for that reason.
+Live

Two security gaps closed

  • Sending from someone else's mailbox. While building item 6, we found that any signed-in account could send email from another person's connected Gmail or Outlook by naming them. That is now refused: you can only send from your own mailbox, or, for an EA, from a client mailbox the client has shared with them.
  • A client's staff could see the client's private calendar. Once a client connected their own calendar, their staff saw it too, against what the client was told. Staff and viewers now see only meetings booked on the account. Staff can also no longer read the account's full task list, only their own tasks.

What's next

  1. Gmail for the client's EA, once Google's security assessment (CASA) is arranged.
  2. Finish Google verification: the demo video and permission notes. This removes Google's "unverified app" warning for clients and the 100-account limit.
  3. Microsoft publisher verification (free), so clients at larger companies can connect Outlook without asking their IT team first.
