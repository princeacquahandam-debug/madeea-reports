1Ready

A QA tester for the Hub

A set of instructions that turns an AI assistant into a careful website tester. Given a site, it maps every page, form and button first, writes a test plan ranked by risk, runs it, and reports each problem with screenshots, exact steps and a severity rating, then recommends what to fix first.

  • Safe by design: it only reads. It never submits, sends, pays or deletes anything on live accounts, never shows a password, and never reports a test as passed when it didn't run it.
  • It was run on the live Hub with four accounts: an assistant, the owner, the client's main login, and a member of the client's own staff.
2Fixed

The three serious problems, and their one cause

The first test found 23 problems, three of them serious:

  • End-of-day reports told a signed-in assistant to "Sign in", so a report could go unfiled. The owner also appeared as a team member for a moment, without the Admin menus, and the Dashboard could drop to zeros.
  • A client staff account flipped between its own staff view and the client owner's portal, without signing out.
  • The client portal could go blank after a reload: still signed in, but showing no assistant and no tasks.

The cause. The Hub asked "who is signed in?" over the internet in 24 separate places, and those checks had to wait in a single queue. When a page opened, they all fired at once. Any that waited more than five seconds gave up and came back with nobody, and the page then acted as if you were signed out or had the lowest role. The same queue held up everything else, which is why the Inbox could sit on "Loading messages…" for 15 seconds.

The fix. All 24 now read the sign-in the browser already holds, which is instant. Pages also wait until they know your role before drawing anything, instead of guessing and correcting a second later. Security is unchanged: the database still checks who you are on every request.

Tested on screen with all three account types before going live: the EOD form shows straight away, the owner's menus are there from the first screen, the staff account stays a staff member, and the client's details survive a reload.

3Live

Ten smaller fixes from the first test

  • The calendar follows your time zone (from Settings, or your computer's), not the zone of whichever calendar synced first.
  • A clear "Page not found" for a mistyped Hub address, instead of a silent jump to the Dashboard. Addresses that only scanners try (such as /.env and /.git) now get a real "not found", and search engines are told to stay out of this private workspace.
  • The address bar matches the screen: signing out moves it to the sign-in address.
  • The starting password on People is hidden as dots until someone clicks Show. Copy still works without showing it.
  • A browser-tab icon, and the Hub's icon image cut from 168 KB to under 4 KB.
  • Forgot password shows only the email box while resetting.
  • The Sign in button has a clear outline when reached with the keyboard.
  • Staff daily-report examples now start with "e.g.", so they no longer look already filled in.
  • No more "added Invalid Date" on the client People page.
  • No loading flash before the "this page isn't available" message.
4Fixed

The re-test, and what it found

The tester went through the live Hub again, with the hardest checks repeated several times. Its verdict moved from "Not ready" to "Ready with fixes":

  • The end-of-day form showed on 6 of 6 fresh sign-ins. The staff account never flipped. The client portal never went blank. Every role landed in the right place, nothing showed across accounts, and the browser reported 0 errors.
  • What remained was mostly "the page loads twice": numbers showing 0 for a second before the real figure arrived. It also reported an empty Client Scoreboard, but checked by hand, the Scoreboard fills in normally; the test had captured it mid-load.

Fixed after the re-test:

  • A grey "—" instead of 0 until each number has loaded, on the Dashboard, Admin panel, Client Scoreboard and the client staff "My work" page. Your title in the sidebar ("Owner · Operations") waits until it's known instead of changing as it loads.
  • The calendar's time zone is a small list (yours, the calendar's, this computer's), not a button that switches on one click.
  • Client staff Settings now say "the account owner" instead of "your assistant", and their avatar initials match top and bottom.
  • A real favicon.ico for browsers and tools that ask for one.
5Live

Client portal addresses, Edit name for clients, and a faster Hub

  • Every client portal section has its own address: /tasks, /people, /settings and so on. Reloading stays on the same page, the browser's Back button moves between sections, and a section can be bookmarked or shared. A staff address typed by a client still goes back to their Overview. The Overview shows "Loading your account…" until its numbers arrive.
  • Edit name for client logins. A login created from an email address was named after it, with no way to change it. Client Settings now has the same Edit name card as the Hub.
  • A lighter Hub. The main file went from 985 KB to 448 KB. React, Supabase, page routing, icons and Madeline's formatting tools are now separate files that download side by side and stay in the browser's cache when the Hub is updated, so returning visitors download far less after each release.

Each change was checked on screen with the client, staff and owner accounts before it went live.
